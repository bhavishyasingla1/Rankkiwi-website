How Instagram Views Change as Accounts Get Bigger

Comparing raw view counts across creators without knowing their follower tier is meaningless. An analysis of Socialinsider's benchmark reveals the exact scaling dynamics across formats.

BS
Bhavishya Singla Creator Research Lead
Published: Oct 5, 2026 • Updated: Oct 5, 2026 • 6 min read
Key Finding Rank Kiwi Analysis of Socialinsider Data

In Socialinsider's benchmark dataset, accounts with 1K–5K followers averaged 527 views on carousels versus 253 on images (a 2.08× advantage). At the 100K–1M tier, average carousel views climbed to 23,760, while Reels views expanded 34.73× from 470 to 16,325.

Core Rank Kiwi Takeaway: A raw view count is only interpretable when you know the creator's scale and format. Creator-relative benchmarking solves this by measuring performance against that specific creator's median rather than population averages.

Executive Summary

Absolute view counts mislead: 5,000 views on a 2,000-follower profile represents a breakout outlier, while the same count on a 500,000-follower account signals an underperformer. Socialinsider's dataset across five audience brackets highlights why content must be benchmarked relative to creator baseline rather than aggregate population numbers.

Rank Kiwi Analysis: Why Follower Scale Changes the Math

1. Micro Accounts Rely Heavily on Format Efficiency

In the 1K–5K bracket, publishing a carousel yielded more than double (2.08×) the views of a single image (527 vs 253). When an account lacks built-in follower momentum, algorithmic distribution mechanics (multi-slide re-delivery and Reels Explore feeds) provide a critical distribution lifeline.

2. Macro Accounts Possess Distribution Inertia

For accounts above 100K followers, format gaps compress. Single images earned 17,150 average views, actually surpassing Reels (16,325) in this sample. Large established accounts command habitual feed attention; their primary distribution driver is existing brand loyalty rather than Explore tab serendipity.

3. The Flaw of Cross-Account Benchmarking

If a researcher evaluates content solely by total views, large accounts will dominate every ranking table, regardless of whether their content is actually creative or mediocre. A post with 10,000 views from a 3K creator is an extraordinary outlier, while that same 10,000 views on a 500K creator represents deep failure.

What This Means vs What This Does NOT Mean

What This Means
  • Raw view counts must always be indexed against account size and content format.
  • Micro-creators should favor multi-slide carousels and Reels to compensate for small subscriber pools.
  • Benchmark a post against the creator's own historical median to spot true creativity.
What This Does NOT Mean
  • It does not mean accounts with 5K followers can never reach 100K views. Outliers break these averages constantly.
  • It does not mean that having 100K followers guarantees 23,000 views every time.
  • It does not represent a proprietary dataset collected directly by Rank Kiwi.

Methodological Limitations

  • Arithmetic Averages (Means): Socialinsider reports arithmetic averages rather than medians. This means top-performing viral outlier posts in each tier skew the reported figures upwards. Typical (median) posts will frequently register below these benchmarks.
  • Sample Scope: The dataset is drawn from accounts monitored through Socialinsider's software, predominantly professional brands, agencies, and full-time creators.
  • Posting Frequency Variance: The benchmark does not control for weekly posting cadence or advertising spend amplification.
