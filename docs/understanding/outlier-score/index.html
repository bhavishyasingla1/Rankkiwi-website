The Quick Answer

Rank Kiwi's Outlier Score reveals how strongly a specific post performed compared with that creator's typical baseline in the analyzed sample.

If a creator typically earns 20,000 views on a Reel and one specific video garners 100,000 views, that video receives an Outlier Score of 5.0×. It earned five times that creator's typical performance.

The Creator-Relative Principle

Why raw view counts fail when researching content formats across different accounts.

Consider two creators in the same fitness niche:

  • Creator A (1,500,000 followers): Typical Reel gets 200,000 views. A post with 100,000 views is an underperformer for them (0.5×).
  • Creator B (25,000 followers): Typical Reel gets 5,000 views. A post with 100,000 views is a massive 20.0× breakout outlier.

Comparing raw views alone would suggest both posts are identical. Relative scoring reveals that Creator B found an extraordinary hook or framing that spread far beyond their follower baseline.

Why Median Beats Average (Mean)

Statistical averages are easily corrupted by extreme spikes; the median reflects true typical performance.

Social media views follow a severe power-law distribution. Suppose you analyze 5 posts from a creator with the following view counts:

Sample Views: [ 10,000 | 12,000 | 14,000 | 16,000 | 500,000 ]
  • Average (Mean): 110,400 views. Notice that 4 out of 5 posts performed far below this average! Using the mean would artificially make normal posts look like failures.
  • Median (Middle Value): 14,000 views. The median captures what the creator actually experiences on a typical Tuesday afternoon.

By comparing the 500,000-view video against the 14,000 median, Rank Kiwi computes a true Outlier Score of ~35.7× without distorting the remaining catalog.

The Mathematical Formula

Rank Kiwi executes the following calculation locally in your browser sandbox:

Outlier Score = Post Views / Creator Median Views

Where:

  • Post Views: The public view or play count extracted for the individual content item.
  • Creator Median Views: The exact numerical median calculated across all items in the current sample batch.

Score Interpretation Tiers

Use these practical benchmark tiers when filtering results:

Score Tier Multiplier Range Meaning Research Takeaway
Below Baseline 0.1× – 0.8× Performed below creator typical median Standard community update or narrow topic
Typical 0.9× – 1.4× Standard expected performance Creator's baseline distribution to existing core audience
Strong 1.5× – 3.5× Solid performer; reached non-followers Compelling hook, strong retention, good topic appeal
High Outlier 3.6× – 6.0× Exceptional breakout performance High algorithmic velocity; examine first 3 seconds closely
Super Outlier 6.1× – 20.0×+ Statistical anomaly Massive cross-niche resonance; study concept and pacing

Sample Size Sensitivity

Why analyzing 25 posts versus 100 posts can slightly adjust the computed score.

Because the Outlier Score is calculated against the median of the analyzed sample, selecting 25 recent posts reflects the creator's recent 30-day baseline, while selecting 100 posts reflects their annual baseline.

If a creator recently gained 500,000 followers, their 25-post baseline will be higher than their 100-post baseline. Both calculations are mathematically correct within their respective sample scopes.

What Outlier Score Does NOT Mean

To maintain analytical integrity, keep these critical boundaries in mind:

  • Not an Algorithm Guarantee: A 10× score does not prove the algorithm will promote that topic again or that copying it guarantees views.
  • Not an Objective Quality Score: An outlier indicates distribution reach, not necessarily production budget or artistic merit. Controversial or polarizing content can generate outlier views.
  • Not Cross-Creator Comparable: A 3× post from a 5,000-follower creator is not "the same" as a 3× post from an international brand. Scores evaluate a creator against themselves.
